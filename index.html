About

Background, current experience, and longer-term direction.

I am an M.S. student in Information Management at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. I received my B.S. in Electronic Information Science & Technology from Nanjing University.

My work connects computational imaging, multimodal sensing, and robot vision. Building on self-supervised RGB–active NIR fusion, I am now developing biologically inspired perception modules that cooperate on motion, geometry, and visual memory through selective information retrieval. In parallel, I am investigating diffusion-based restoration for low-light scenes where both sensing modalities become unreliable.

I am interested in systems that remain reliable when visual inputs are incomplete or degraded, and in understanding how sensing, geometry, and perception should be modeled together.

Selected Research

The projects in which I have developed the strongest relevant experience.

Bio-Inspired Multi-Agent Perception for RGB–NIR Robot Vision

Ongoing Research · Modular sensing and geometric perception

  • Developing a modular visual system combining lightweight RGB–active NIR fusion (LaminaFuse) with task-specialized, insect-inspired perception components for low-light temporal cues, motion, depth, and visual memory.
  • Designed a structured shared perceptual memory with on-demand retrieval between task-specific modules, and prototyped depth–pose feedback.
  • Evaluating geometric accuracy, temporal consistency, and compute cost, including comparisons against simpler same-budget feedback baselines. The complete multi-agent system remains under development.
Sep. 2026–Present

Geometry-Guided Diffusion Restoration for RGB–NIR Imaging

Ongoing Research · Low-light image restoration and geometric consistency

  • Developing a region-aware diffusion restoration method for scenes where RGB underexposure and active-NIR saturation coincide, causing complementary sensors to fail in the same areas.
  • Investigating confidence masks and depth- or geometry-guided constraints to restore degraded regions while maintaining scene structure.
  • Designing evaluations of downstream depth, feature matching, and temporal consistency; the method is in active development, with experiments underway.
2026–Present

Self-Supervised RGB–Active NIR Fusion for Geometric Perception

Completed Research · Collaboration with Prof. Guoyu Lu

  • Developed a lightweight single-channel fusion representation for registered RGB and active NIR using pixel-wise mixing, reprojection constraints, and an exposure-aware reliability gate.
  • Evaluated stereo depth, feature matching, SfM/SLAM diagnostics, detection, and efficiency; observed 79.36 FPS and 560 MB peak memory for the evaluated configuration.
  • Further robustness experiments focus on challenging illumination, calibration variation, and sensor degradation. The associated manuscript is unpublished.
Nov. 2025–May 2026

Per-Pixel Illumination Estimation from HR-RGB and LR-HSI

Student Researcher, CITE Lab, Nanjing University

  • Developed a multi-sensor pipeline combining high-resolution RGB and low-resolution hyperspectral measurements for per-pixel illumination estimation.
  • Reproduced baseline results and evaluated robustness across multiple spatial downsampling scales and fusion settings.
