About

Background, current experience, and longer-term direction.

I will begin the M.S. in Information Management program at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign in August 2026. I received my B.S. in Electronic Information Science & Technology from Nanjing University.

My research interests include computational imaging, multispectral and spectral imaging, statistical image reconstruction, multimodal sensing and perception, human-perception-informed imaging, and sensor calibration for real imaging systems. My recent work has centered on multimodal sensing and computer vision, with the most extensive experience in self-supervised RGB–active NIR fusion, as well as additional work in RGB–hyperspectral fusion, sparse LiDAR supervision, and robotic navigation.

I am particularly interested in research that connects real imaging systems with computational reconstruction: understanding how sensors measure the physical world, how statistical or learned priors compensate for incomplete measurements, and how reconstructed images relate to human visual perception.

Long-term research direction

I am interested in combining physical imaging models, spectral representations, statistical learning, and carefully collected human perceptual data to study perceptually equivalent image reconstruction and the limitations of conventional cameras.

Selected Research

The projects in which I have developed the strongest relevant experience.

Self-Supervised RGB–Active NIR Fusion for Visual Perception

Project Lead and First Author · Advisor: G. Lu

  • Developed a lightweight single-channel fusion framework for pixel-aligned RGB and active NIR using pixel-wise mixing, stereo reprojection, and an exposure-aware reliability gate.
  • Evaluated the fused representation across five downstream tasks—object detection, stereo depth estimation, feature matching, Structure from Motion (SfM), and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM). The model runs at 79.36 FPS with 560 MB peak memory.
  • Currently extending the study with expanded training and evaluation protocols, robustness analysis under calibration perturbations, ablation studies, and experiments under challenging illumination conditions.
Nov. 2025–Present

Per-Pixel Illumination Estimation from HR-RGB and LR-HSI

Student Researcher, CITE Lab, Nanjing University

  • Developed a multi-sensor pipeline combining high-resolution RGB and low-resolution hyperspectral measurements for per-pixel illumination estimation.
  • Reproduced baseline results and evaluated robustness across multiple spatial downsampling scales and fusion settings.
Jun. 2025–Oct. 2025
